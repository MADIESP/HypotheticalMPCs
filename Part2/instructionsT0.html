Explanation of the hypothetical scenario

Suppose that today you learn that you and your household will receive an unexpected one-time payment of $1,000. This payment will not be taxed and will arrive in your checking account within a few days (for example as a stimulus check, tax refund, bonus, inheritance, gift, or lottery win).

We'll ask you to think about how you and your household would use this extra money over the next three months, compared with what you would have done if you had not received the payment.

In particular, with this extra money, you and your household could:

  • Change your debt repayment

    You could change the amount of debt you repay (e.g., credit card debt, mortgages, loans, unpaid bills).

  • Change your spending

    You could change your consumption of nondurable goods (e.g., food, clothes, entertainment), durable goods (e.g., vehicles, furniture), and services (e.g., babysitting, house-cleaning).

Any amount not used would be considered savings. This money would be set aside for future use, for instance by depositing it in checking, savings, or pension accounts, or by purchasing financial assets.
