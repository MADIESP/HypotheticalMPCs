Welcome to Part 2!

In this part, we ask you to imagine a hypothetical situation and tell us how you and your household would respond.

Suppose that today you learn that you and your household will receive an unexpected one-time payment of $1,000. This payment will not be taxed and will arrive in your checking account within a few days (for example as a stimulus check, tax refund, bonus, inheritance, gift, or lottery win).

Please think about how you and your household would use this extra money over the next three months, compared to what you would have done without receiving the payment.

In particular, you and your household could use this money to:

  • Change your spending

    You could change your consumption of nondurable goods (e.g., food, clothes, vacation, entertainment), durable goods (e.g., vehicles, furniture, household appliances), and services (e.g., babysitting, house-cleaning).

  • Change your investments and assets

    You could change the amount of investment you make (e.g., real estate, investments in your own business, crypto) and the amount of assets that you hold (e.g., stocks, bonds, retirement or savings accounts).

  • Change your debt repayment

    You could change the amount you repay on revolving credit card debt, mortgages, loans, or unpaid bills.

  • Change your uptake of new debt

    You could change the amount of new debt you take on (e.g., credit card debt, mortgages, loans, unpaid bills) to finance extra spending, investment, or other purchases.

  • Change your labor income

    You could change your labor earnings by changing the number of work hours, work opportunities, or gig economy jobs that you accept.

Any amount not used would stay in your checking account for future use.
